Resources

Where to get help right now, and two short exercises for when things feel like too much.

If you need to talk to someone now

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US). Call 988 or text 988, any time of day. You can also chat online. It's free and confidential, and you can reach out if you're worried about someone else.
  • Crisis Text Line. Text HOME to 741741 to reach a trained counselor by text.
  • If you or someone near you is in immediate danger, call 911.
  • Outside the US, findahelpline.com lists free services by country.

Exercises

4-7-8 breathing (about a minute)

A long, slow exhale can take the edge off anxiety. Sit or lie down somewhere comfortable.

  1. Breathe out fully through your mouth.
  2. Close your mouth and breathe in quietly through your nose for a count of 4.
  3. Hold your breath for a count of 7.
  4. Breathe out through your mouth for a count of 8.
  5. That's one cycle. Do four in a row.

If holding makes you lightheaded, count faster. Keeping the 4, 7, 8 ratio matters more than the exact seconds.

5-4-3-2-1 grounding (2 to 3 minutes)

Useful when your thoughts are racing or you feel far away from what's around you. Go slowly and name each thing, out loud or in your head.

  1. 5 things you can see.
  2. 4 things you can touch, like your feet on the floor or the fabric of your sleeve.
  3. 3 things you can hear.
  4. 2 things you can smell. If nothing stands out, think of two smells you like.
  5. 1 thing you can taste, or one food you'd like to eat.
Finding ongoing support
  • Someone you trust. A short text is enough to start: "I've been having a rough time. Can we talk this week?"
  • A therapist. The Psychology Today directory lets you filter by location, insurance and specialty. Your insurer can also send you a list of in-network providers.
  • Your school or job. Most colleges offer free counseling, and many employers have an Employee Assistance Program with a few free sessions.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline. 1-800-662-4357, free and open 24/7, for referrals to local treatment and support groups.
